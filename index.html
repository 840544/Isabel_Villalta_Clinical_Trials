About

From the bench to the trial file

If you are here, you are probably interested in the person behind the experience. So, let me briefly tell you how I got here.

My journey into clinical research began with Biotechnology, where I developed a strong scientific foundation and discovered how much I enjoyed connecting laboratory science with real clinical questions. During my degree, I had the opportunity to spend a month in the Clinical Genetics Department at Hospital Universitario Miguel Servet, gaining my first exposure to clinical diagnostics and molecular and cytogenetic techniques.

That experience sparked my interest in the clinical side of science. Building on it, I later completed a rotation at the FEETEG specialised laboratory as part of my clinical research training, where I was introduced to the diagnosis and follow-up of rare lysosomal disorders. These experiences helped me understand how laboratory data can ultimately contribute to patient care.

I then took the next step by completing the academic component of my MSc in Clinical Trial Monitoring at UNIR. My Master’s thesis, in particular, allowed me to put this knowledge into practice by designing a complete Phase IIb clinical trial protocol, from study design and endpoints to monitoring, statistics, and translational biomarkers.

Today, I am bringing that training into the clinical research environment as a Clinical Research and Study Coordination Intern at Hospital Quirónsalud Zaragoza, supporting Phase I–III trials in haematology and rare diseases. Here, I am gaining hands-on experience with clinical documentation, data management, query resolution, patient visit coordination, safety documentation, biological sample logistics, and Investigator Site File maintenance, always working within GCP and protocol requirements.

Along the way, I have developed the qualities I believe are essential in clinical operations: attention to detail, organisation, adaptability, and clear communication. Most importantly, I have developed a genuine interest in the people behind the data and in the processes that make high-quality clinical research possible.

And this is only the beginning.

I am now looking for my first full-time opportunity in Clinical Operations or Study Coordination, where I can keep learning, contribute to meaningful clinical research, and grow alongside an experienced multidisciplinary team within a CRO or pharmaceutical environment.

Thanks for stopping by — I hope you enjoy exploring the rest of my journey.

Clinical Experience

Hematology and rare diseases, in practice

Hematology

4 conditions

Myelofibrosis

A chronic myeloproliferative neoplasm driven by JAK-STAT dysregulation, causing progressive bone marrow fibrosis, cytopenias, and splenomegaly. The most protocol-intensive area of my internship: I support Phase I–III studies spanning a telomerase inhibitor in JAK-inhibitor-refractory disease, an XPO1 inhibitor combined with ruxolitinib, an oral MDM2 inhibitor restoring p53-mediated apoptosis, a subcutaneous TGF-β ligand trap for anemia-dominant profiles, and a BTK inhibitor for patients ineligible for standard JAK inhibition.

Polycythemia Vera

A clonal myeloproliferative disorder caused by JAK2 mutations driving excess red blood cell production and a heightened risk of thrombotic events. Trial protocols in this area focus on long-term disease control and event prevention, with close haematological monitoring at every scheduled visit.

Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

A clonal myeloid malignancy defined by the BCR-ABL1 fusion oncogene and its constitutively active tyrosine kinase. I support a Phase 1/2 study evaluating a next-generation allosteric BCR-ABL1 inhibitor — designed to overcome resistance mutations that limit earlier-generation TKIs — in previously treated patients in chronic phase.

Mastocytosis

A clonal disorder of pathological mast cell accumulation, most often driven by KIT D816V mutations. I support a Phase 2 trial evaluating an oral BTK inhibitor in patients with indolent systemic mastocytosis — a population with significant unmet need and limited approved therapeutic options.

Rare Diseases

3 conditions

Gaucher Disease

The most prevalent lysosomal storage disorder, caused by glucocerebrosidase deficiency and leading to lipid accumulation in the liver, spleen, and bone marrow. My exposure spans three therapeutic paradigms: a long-term observational registry following patients on established therapy; a Phase 1/2 first-in-human rAAV9-based gene therapy trial; and an early-phase adenoviral vector gene therapy study targeting hepatic glucocerebrosidase expression.

Fabry Disease

An X-linked lysosomal storage disorder caused by alpha-galactosidase A deficiency, with systemic involvement affecting the kidneys, heart, and nervous system. I support the Phase 3 MODIFY trial evaluating lucerastat — an oral substrate reduction therapy — in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design with a long-term open-label extension.

Lysosomal Disorders — FEETEG Lab

During a rotation at the FEETEG specialist diagnostic laboratory, I assisted with enzyme activity testing and biochemical follow-up across four rare lysosomal conditions: ASMD (formerly Niemann-Pick A/B), Niemann-Pick type C, Lysosomal Acid Lipase Deficiency (LAL-D), and Fabry Disease — a setting where biochemical precision at diagnosis directly shapes long-term treatment decisions.

On my role As a Clinical Research & Study Coordination and Clinical Research trainee, I support these trials under supervision — resolving data queries, assisting with source data verification, coordinating visits and shipments, and maintaining eCRFs. I don't run these trials; I help keep them on track.
